### Settlement cheat table

| Settlement | Where | Whose | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jamestown / Williamsburg | VA | English | 1607, first permanent English settlement; Williamsburg the later capital |
| Plymouth | MA coast | English | 1620, Pilgrims and the Mayflower Compact |
| Boston, MBC | MA | English | 1630, Massachusetts Bay Colony's Puritan hub |
| Providence | RI | English | 1636, Roger Williams's haven for religious dissenters |
| New Haven | CT | English | 1638, strict Puritan port town |
| New York / New Amsterdam | NY harbor | Dutch, then English | Dutch 1626; England seized and renamed it in 1664 |
| Albany | Upper Hudson | Dutch, then English | began as a Dutch fur-trading post |
| Philadelphia | PA | English | 1682, Penn's Quaker city |
| Baltimore | MD | English | Chesapeake tobacco port |
| New Bern | NC | English | Swiss and German settlers on the Carolina coast |
| Charleston | SC | English | 1670, the great southern port city |
| Savannah | GA | English | 1733, Oglethorpe's planned city in the buffer colony |
| Quebec | St. Lawrence | French | 1608, Champlain's capital of New France |
| New Orleans | Mississippi mouth | French | 1718, controls Mississippi River trade |
| St. Augustine | FL | Spanish | 1565, oldest European city in the future U.S. |

<Callout type="definition" title="The Mason-Dixon Line, since it always gets asked">
  Surveyed in the 1760s to settle a border dispute between <strong>Pennsylvania and Maryland</strong> (the green line on your map). Decades later it became shorthand for the divide between free North and slave South, but for this unit it is simply the PA-MD boundary.
</Callout>
